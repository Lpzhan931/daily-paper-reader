# ABSTRACT 

Numerical execution paths can change greedy output; a mismatch alone does not predict the remaining trajectory. On the tested B570 stack, we select a draft-free target-only micro-batch-one predictor using exposed discovery data, then freeze complete generated-ID predictions before reserved live speculation. It matches 72/72 capped sequences on 24 repeat-stable prompts, including all 12 runs of four reference-divergent prompts; the same-set reference matches 60/72. The predictor is sufficient for this path, but it neither restores qualification nor identifies a unique numerical cause. Separate B570 same-state forks test intervention effects; RTX proposal/replay programs test reproduction while sharing numerical routines. We assess cost separately. Newly direct-ID-qualified RTX serialization is slower within its instrumented post-prefill boundary (0.646 × target/serialized latency). At fixed measured progress and target work, 358H remains below parity even with draft and residual costs removed. Qualified M4 Guard beats target-only but loses to fixed speculation. These results give testable diagnoses and engineering decisions about lossless compatibility and complete cost on the measured paths; they establish no portable decoding or control gain. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Speculative decoding uses cheap proposals to amortize tar-get work (Leviathan et al., 2023). Prior consumer-hardware measurements show both speedups and slowdowns, as well as greedy argmax changes when the target’s batch shape changes (Chordiya, 2026). We ask whether an execution-path hypothesis can predict complete, unseen speculative output, including departures from the intended reference. We test this on one B570 Vulkan stack. A draft-free target-only UB1 predictor is selected after 36/36 discov-ery matches; its 72 complete ID arrays are then frozen before reserved live outcomes. They match 72/72 runs on 24 repeat-stable prompts, including four reference-divergent prompts (12 runs); the UB128 reference matches 60/72. A wrong later token would falsify the prediction. The result shows that target-only execution suffices to predict this path, without identifying a unique kernel cause. Accurate predic-tion does not make this path a lossless replacement for the reference. Two supporting studies examine causality and transfer. A separately selected B570 cohort compares identical fork states: scores change but immediate decisions do not, while some later trajectories differ under a persistent alternate policy. RTX’s target-only H1 fails discovery. A later, in-dependently orchestrated P/R program reproduces reserved live paths using sealed proposals and shared numerical rou-tines. It uses drafting to construct predictions and does not show that proposal/history inputs are necessary. The orig-inal draft-free prediction leaves these causal and transfer questions unresolved. A compatible path still needs a separate cost test. Original direct-ID gates qualify RTX serialization but leave B570 blocked; they do not certify historical timing. A new RTX study qualifies its own timer path, yet serialization is slower. On 358H, measured target work prevents arithmetic parity at fixed progress even with draft cost eliminated. On M4, hindsight selection between fixed modes leaves little oppor-tunity, and Guard adds no aggregate benefit. Each case uses its own frozen comparator, so these results do not rank the hardware. Our three contributions are (1) direct-ID path qualification and matched instrumented cost on a newly qualified RTX path, where compatibility does not bring a speedup; (2) prospective B570 full-trajectory prediction, with separate intervention and RTX program tests of its causal and transfer limits; and (3) complete 358H interface accounting and qualified M4 fixed-policy comparisons that show the limits of draft-only and continuation-control improvements. 1arXiv:2610.03228v1 [cs.PF] 2 Oct 2026 Execution-Path Qualification in Speculative Decoding

Acceptance, isolated draft throughput and target-forward batching measure different boundaries. None alone mea-sures the complete speculative loop. We distinguish them using an output contract and complete-cost accounting. 

2.1 A path-qualified output contract 

Let k be the maximum draft proposal width, UB the tar-get micro-batch capacity ( nubatch ), and EOG a vocabulary end-of-generation ID. A greedy speculative round verifies draft proposals, commits the matching prefix and a correc-tion or bonus when budget permits, then removes incon-sistent suffix state. Our strict contract requires committed generated IDs to equal the matched target-only greedy ref-erence under the frozen configuration. We can therefore test lossless compatibility with a specified deployed greedy reference for each model, runtime, path and workload. A failed pair blocks a workload-global optimization claim; a small passing probe only establishes eligibility for broader testing. Capped ID equality does not measure functional HumanEval correctness or pass@ k.Historical text-path agreement (H) compares arrays recon-structed from parsed CLI text. Direct-ID qualification (Q) compares captured IDs against the study’s frozen reference. Frozen prediction equality (P) compares a sealed prediction with reserved live IDs. A predictor can match a reference-divergent path, so P does not imply Q. Same-state inter-vention separately compares scores, immediate decisions and subsequent trajectories; these endpoints must not be interchanged. 

2.2 Evidence classes Primary results answer a frozen question after its applicable gate; secondary results retain their registered or post-study follow-up scope. Mechanism-supported results use path controls or measured regions. Diagnostic values describe blocked or restricted paths. Historical RTX controls remain text-path secondary evidence and B570 timing diagnostic, irrespective of later direct-ID results.

5.3 RTX independent proposal and replay prediction 

The separately frozen RTX H1 discovery tests target-only UB2 against native batched live and matches only 24/36, failing its 36/36 gate. No hypothesis is selected, its H2 interface is unavailable, and reserved execution is zero. This failure remains distinct from subsequent V2 work. V2 implements independent draft-only P and target-only R processes. P constructs proposals using H2’s own prior feed-back; R performs verification, acceptance/correction/bonus and rollback. A fresh R ONLY loads only target weights and consumes the sealed tape sequentially, computing its own decisions without future live answers. Original CLI live generates its own draft and imports no tape. Code, model-loading and RPC records support application/process/API independence; all programs share llama.cpp/GGML/CUDA numerical routines. Discovery H2/live and H2/R ONLY both pass 36/36. Fol-lowing F0/F1/F2 freezes, complete predictions and tapes precede reserved live on another 24-task set × three: 288 executions across reference, H2, R ONLY and live. H2/live, R ONLY/live and H2/R ONLY each match 72/72, whereas this study’s UB1 reference/live matches 60/72. Four tasks (/17, /33, /14, /22) diverge in all repetitions. Live endpoints contain 63 earlier-EOG runs and nine capped runs; “com-plete” does not mean all reach 128. The recorded proposals and target path suffice to reconstruct these trajectories; H2 construction still uses drafting. This establishes program-level prospective reproduction. No target-only UB2 com-petitor was tested on this same V2 reserved set, so it does not establish proposal/history necessity. # 6 FROM MEASURED CONSTRAINTS TO 

# RUNTIME DECISIONS 

Batch-shape sensitivity is already known. B570 adds a prospective test that could fail on any reserved ID. The four correctly predicted non-reference trajectories show that the predictor does more than echo the reference. This path must still be rejected as a lossless replacement for that ref-erence. The fork’s unchanged sampled decisions limit the causal explanation, and RTX’s failed H1 prevents treating the target-only rule as portable. Its later P/R reproduction uses a different input contract. Cost measurements also constrain the available changes. New RTX serialization passes its own direct-ID gate but is slower within its measured boundary. On 358H, removing all draft and residual cost still cannot reach parity at fixed progress and measured target work. An improvement would therefore require changing progress or target work; moving or accelerating the draft alone is insufficient. The M4 study below tests whether continuation control improves a qual-ified fixed path. These decisions depend on each study’s comparator and establish neither a common numerical cause nor a performance law across stacks.

7.1 An intervention stopped by qualification 

The 358H component measurements motivated persistent draft state and a wider target execution setting. The im-plementation ran the target and draft backends in separate processes. Synchronous draft calls, including communi-cation and state work, remained inside the target worker’s post-prefill timer. The implementation then had to pass cor-rectness qualification. CPU persistent-state restore/reconcile correctness could not be established under the tested repre-sentation; the Optimum NPU state-capable path was unavail-able with the frozen dynamic-shape IR. These are imple-mentation boundaries, not evidence that the devices cannot retain state. The target setting n ubatch=2 (UB2) passed the small gate but failed full qualification. Correctness-only follow-up controls on Qwen3-8B Q8 0, llama.cpp b10839, and Vulkan0 reproduced different greedy tokens under UB1 and UB2 even with identical exact prefix tokens. Request-order and separate-process controls reproduced the target-only divergence. Replaying frozen proposals also reproduced the speculative divergence without live NPU draft infer-ence. The latter depended on earlier target execution his-tory; the controls found no position drift or specific rollback-boundary bug. The responsible floating-point or backend operation remains unidentified. Under this frozen tested target stack, UB2 did not preserve the strict direct-ID output contract. 7.2 A prospective cost guard on M4 

The M4 experiment asks whether a frozen rule based on already-realized complete cost can control continuation while preserving exact output and profitable execution against target-only. It does not assume that adding con-trol improves on fixed speculation. We retain the Qwen3-8B/0.6B Q8 0 pair on llama.cpp Metal, fixed k = 2 , greedy generation, and a 128-token cap. Guard begins on the same speculative path as Always-Spec and can fall back perma-nently to target-only within a request. It changes neither proposal width nor device placement. At each eligible completed round, Guard divides all elapsed charged generation cost by committed output progress C.This count includes the initial target seed and committed correction or bonus tokens, but excludes rejected propos-als. Guard continues if this cumulative cost per token is at most (1 − m)Rref , a target-only reference frozen from calibration; otherwise it falls back. If a decision is reached with zero committed progress, Guard also falls back. Be-fore each control decision, invalid candidate suffix state is removed from the target and draft contexts, preserving committed history for target-only continuation. The enclos-ing post-prefill clock includes generation-loop initialization, seed-token sampling, drafting, target work, synchronization, state reconciliation, control, and transition costs. Model loading, sampler/speculator setup, initial prompt prefill, and the speculative begin hook precede the timer; the result is not whole-request latency. The reference is the median across calibration prompts of each prompt’s median target latency divided by its median output-token count. The preregistered candidate grid varies the first decision round and margin. Selection minimizes calibration geometric-mean latency relative to the offline minimum of target-only and Always-Spec among exact can-didates, with frozen tie-breaks. The selected rule uses mar-gin 0 and first decides after 8 completed speculative rounds; it then decides after each later round while speculation con-tinues. A request ending earlier makes no decision. The online policy receives neither task/family labels nor future output length, current-request target-only latency, or oracle outcomes.

# 8 RELATED WORK 

Performance and heterogeneous execution. Speculative decoding amortizes target computation under its algorith-mic assumptions (Leviathan et al., 2023). Prior work al-ready documents verification and workload limits: Liu et al. (2026b) study verification dominance and batch effects in vLLM; Kim et al. (2026) account for conditional execution cost and runtime control; Lyu et al. (2026) derive edge– cloud profitability conditions. On consumer Apple silicon, Chordiya (2026) report gains and slowdowns across five backend configurations, diagnose ineffective batch-parallel verification in one quantized Metal path, test output agree-ment, and observe target-only batch-shape argmax changes. Slowdown and correctness gating have already been studied, as has heterogeneous placement: Dovetail, DuoDecoding, and Mirror-SD study GPU/CPU or GPU/NPU paths (Zhang et al., 2025; Lv et al., 2025; Bhendawade et al., 2025); Ruiz y Mesa et al. (2026) combine an analytical cost model with compiler-oriented CPU/GPU partitioning, and Lever jointly optimizes draft, verification, and CPU/NPU execution for a smartphone target (Wang et al., 2026). State reuse has prior art in compressed draft KV memory (Yuan et al., 2026). Our 358H study measures one interface that combines re-play and proposal; it establishes no general placement or persistence result. 

Numerical paths and output contracts. Zhang et al. (2026) distinguish ragged-batch synchronization errors from residual numerical mismatches. Yuan et al. (2025) study output variation across system configurations, and LLM-42 addresses deterministic inference through verified specu-lation (Gond et al., 2026). Numerical path dependence is not new here. We prospectively qualify specific RTX/B570 paths against captured generated IDs, separately from histor-ical text-path timing. For B570, H1 was selected after 36/36 exposed discovery matches, then complete predictions were frozen before 72 reserved live executions; four prompts 9Execution-Path Qualification in Speculative Decoding 

match the non-reference prediction in all 12 runs, ruling out a predictor that simply echoes the reference on this set. H2 was unrun in that original B570 tree. Subsequent same-state B570 forks test sampled immediate and continuation effects separately; RTX’s H1 fails, while its independently frozen P/R replay study tests program-level prospective re-production using shared numerical routines. This tests path admissibility and predictive sufficiency, not a new numerical phenomenon, unique kernel cause, or competing reserved predictor. 

Adaptive speculation and the study boundary. Tur-boSpec adjusts speculation for serving goodput (Liu et al., 2025); BanditSpec selects configurations online (Hou et al., 2025); ACL HeteroSpec adapts verification from contextual entropy (Liu et al., 2026a). Adaptive control is established prior work. Our M4 Guard is a qualified runtime control test, not a new policy advantage: it beats target-only, but fixed speculation is faster in the post-study paired secondary comparison. Our measurements test prospective direct-ID qualification, frozen trajectory prediction, sampled interven-tion effects and realized cost on qualified paths. 

# 9 LIMITATIONS 

The studies cover a narrow Qwen3 family, greedy capped outputs and independently frozen consumer stacks. Hard-ware, backend, OS and quantization vary together, so the results cannot establish hardware rankings, population fail-ure rates, functional HumanEval pass@ k or production-load behavior. Strict-ID compatibility is conditional on each ref-erence, and historical text-path timing remains distinct. The new RTX timer includes debug logging and lacks a qualified batched comparison or logging-free deployment estimate. B570 H1 has 24 reserved prompts and four reference-divergent trajectories on one stack. The fork samples one checkpoint per new run; persistent UB128 continuation is not a single-block restoration or an explanation of the old four tasks. RTX H1 fails discovery, and V2 H2 uses drafting and shared numerical routines. Without a same-set target-only UB2 competitor, proposal/history necessity is unknown. No unique operation or portable numerical cause is identified. Prompt intervals exclude session and recalibration variation. Events/repetitions are correlated; exposure exclusion covers retained records only. Hashes identify bytes and host/server records report order; neither anonymous ordinals nor Git alone authenticates original absolute time or omitted bina-ries. 358H runtime raw traces and exact old sequence ZIP are unavailable; preserved arrays and processed cost records remain independently checkable. Its timing region com-bines reconstruction/replay with proposal, so their separate costs are unavailable. The zero-draft bound holds only at fixed measured progress and target work. M4 Guard/Always is post-study secondary, not the regis-tered primary endpoint. New family-stratified sensitivity and fixed-mode opportunity retain that status. The oracle chooses two measured modes with hindsight, not arbitrary switching policies. The M4 metadata defect retains original error and stopped markers. Runtime energy and 358H persis-tence gains remain unmeasured. The energy domains differ, and M4’s corrected synthetic ceiling was not executed. 

# 10 CONCLUSION 

On the tested B570 stack, frozen draft-free UB1 predic-tions match 72/72 reserved capped sequences, including 12 runs of four reference-divergent prompts; reference/live agreement is 60/72. The test predicts complete future trajec-tories rather than only observing path sensitivity, but lossless compatibility remains blocked. Same-state forks examine intervention effects, and independently orchestrated RTX replay tests program reproduction. The cost comparisons lead to specific decisions: qualified RTX serialization is slower within its instrumented boundary; fixed-progress 358H target work prevents parity even at zero draft/residual cost; M4 Guard loses to fixed speculation despite preserv-ing IDs. Each conclusion is limited to its tested reference and execution path.
